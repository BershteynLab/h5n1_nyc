Parameter table

Exhaustive inventory of the NYC H5N1 model. Maps and the scenario comparison use the scenario midpoint. Where a literature range exists it is shown in brackets after the midpoint (e.g. 1.25 [1.2, 1.4]); that is a literature bound from parameters.csv, not a statistical confidence interval. The Ref column is separate: [1], [2] are citations in the list under the table. A dash means that row has no citation on file yet. Shiny sliders explore around the midpoint and do not change the static HTML results until the maps are re-run. Open the Shiny app to vary slider parameters. What changed since the previous hub table is on a separate page. The previous table is kept at parameters_previous.html.

References

One number can cover more than one paper. Where mild, moderate, and severe use different sources, the cell lists all of them and the entry says which scenario each cluster supports. Rows with a dash still need a citation: symptomatic fraction, every length-of-stay row (including step-down), vaccine coverage, the share of hospitalized patients who are treated, the ZIP burden weights, and the two reporting sliders.

  1. Mild R₀. Biggerstaff et al., BMC Infect Dis 2014, systematic review, seasonal about 1.28 (1.19–1.37). PMC4169819. Chowell, Miller, and Viboud, Epidemiol Infect 2008, United States, France, and Australia, about 1.3 (1.2–1.4). Epidemiol Infect 2008. Estimates across 119 countries, about 1.23 (1.1–1.6). Int J Infect Dis 2022.
  2. Moderate R₀. Biggerstaff et al. 2014 (same review as [1]), pandemic estimate about 1.46 (1.30–1.70). Fraser et al., PLoS Med 2009, early 2009 H1N1 in Mexico, about 1.8. PLoS Med 2009. Systematic review of 24 studies, about 1.5 (1.2–2.3). PMC4942041.
  3. Severe R₀, ancestral COVID-19. Meta-analysis of 29 studies, about 2.87 (2.39–3.34). PLoS One 2020. Twelve studies, January–February 2020, about 2.79 (1.63–3.95). PMC7074654. Systematic review of 23 studies, about 3.32 (2.81–3.82). PMC7280807.
  4. Mild incubation. Lessler et al., Lancet Infect Dis 2009, systematic review, about 1.9 days (1.8–2.0). PMC4327893. WHO Writing Group, about 2 days (1–4). PMC3291414.
  5. Moderate incubation. Lessler et al., N Engl J Med 2009, 2009 H1N1 in a New York City high school, about 1.4 days. NEJM 2009. 316 laboratory-confirmed 2009 H1N1 cases in Canada, about 4.3 days. PMC2817319. CDC, 2009 H1N1 year in review, typically 2–3 days (range 1–7). CDC 2009 review.
  6. Severe incubation, ancestral COVID-19. 181 cases, January–February 2020, about 5.1 days (4.5–5.8). PMC7081172. Meta-analysis of 8 studies, about 5.8 days (5–6.7). BMJ Open 2020. Systematic review of 41 studies, about 5 days (4–6). PMC7231098.
  7. Infectious period. Hong Kong community study of influenza A and B: most viral shedding 2–3 days after onset. PMID 20377412. CDC Yellow Book: infectious from the day before onset to about 5–7 days after. CDC Yellow Book. CDC 2009 H1N1 year in review: 5–7 days after onset (same page as [5]). Ancestral SARS-CoV-2 generation interval about 5.2 days. PMID 32372755. Pooled mean generation time about 4.95 days. PMID 37775772.
  8. CDC preliminary influenza burden, 2024–2025: about 51 million illnesses, 23 million medical visits, 710,000 hospitalizations, and 45,000 deaths. Mild hospitalization uses this season. Visits divided by illnesses is about 45%, which is the care-seeking piece of c_out. CDC 2024–2025 burden.
  9. ICU among hospitalized influenza patients. CDC MMWR: about 16.8% of patients hospitalized with influenza required ICU care. MMWR 2025. The literature sheet also points at FluSurv-NET laboratory-confirmed influenza hospitalizations, United States, 2010–2023.
  10. Case fatality, mild end. CDC estimated influenza mortality, 2023–2024: 1.9 per 100,000 cases at ages 18–49 and 9.1 per 100,000 at ages 50–64. CDC 2023–2024 burden.
  11. Vaccine effectiveness. Narrative review of influenza vaccination and illness severity. PMID 34090700. CDC interim estimates of seasonal influenza vaccine effectiveness, about 38–41% against hospitalization under age 18. MMWR. SHIVERS, New Zealand, vaccine effectiveness in hospital. PMID 30077480.
  12. Care-seeking. Biggerstaff et al., MMWR 2011: during 2009 H1N1, 40% of adults and 56% of children with influenza-like illness sought care. MMWR 2011. The 45% used in c_out is visits divided by illnesses in the 2024–2025 CDC burden estimate ([8]).
  13. Early presentation. Biggerstaff et al., J Infect Dis 2014: about 35% of US adults with influenza-like illness sought care within 2 days of onset. PMID 24731959. The evidence note still needs a full-text check of whether 35% is among all illness or only among people who sought care. c_out multiplies 45% by this 35%.
  14. Oseltamivir and admission. The 21% central reduction is a planning value inside a 0–25% range. Hsu et al., Ann Intern Med 2012, hospitalization OR 0.75 (0.66–0.89) in high-risk patients. Qiu et al., Infect Dis (Lond) 2015, hospitalization RR 0.79 (0.68–0.90). Hanula et al., JAMA Intern Med 2024, overall RR 0.77 (0.47–1.27), not significant; high-risk point estimate 0.65. Dobson et al., Lancet 2015, RR 0.37 (0.17–0.81) in the influenza-infected population, is the optimistic bound. Jefferson and colleagues (Cochrane, 2014) is the no-effect bound.
  15. Oseltamivir and death in hospital. Muthuri et al., Lancet Respir Med 2014 (PRIDE): any neuraminidase inhibitor versus none, adjusted OR 0.81 (0.70–0.93), about a 19% reduction, in hospitalized 2009 H1N1. PMID 24815805.
  16. Baloxavir, and the combination row. Ison et al., Lancet Infect Dis 2020 (CAPSTONE-2): hospitalization RR 0.60 (0.14–2.48) versus placebo in high-risk outpatients, with very few events. The table sets both baloxavir reductions equal to oseltamivir as a working assumption, not as that trial’s point estimate. Kumar et al., Lancet Infect Dis 2022 (FLAGSTONE): baloxavir added to a neuraminidase inhibitor in hospitalized patients; day-28 death RR 0.37 (0.11–1.27). The combination death row uses that trial as the upper sensitivity.
  17. In-hospital death, mild and moderate. Taylor et al., Epidemiol Infect 2016, Canadian Nosocomial Infection Surveillance Program: 30-day mortality about 2.6–4.2% in non-ICU adults and about 21–33% in ICU adults with laboratory-confirmed influenza. PMC4762243.
  18. Severe in-hospital death is a planning step up from [17], informed by the Northwell spring 2020 COVID-19 series (Richardson et al., JAMA 2020). That series is right-censored, so the derived ward and ICU death shares are inflated. JAMA 2020.
  19. U.S. Census Bureau, 2020 Census. New York City population 8,584,629.
  20. New York State Department of Health hospital census dated 10 July 2026, the geocoded campus file in this project (46 acute-care sites and staffed beds). Ward occupancy 79.8% and the ICU occupancy in the same working file are stakeholder-provided New York City values, not a separate publication.
